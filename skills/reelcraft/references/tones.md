# Tone presets

A preset shapes energy, pacing, and text style. Freeform text passed to `--tone`
overrides or refines the closest preset rather than replacing this list.

## Marketing mode

| Preset | Energy | Voice | Pacing | Use when |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `default` | Playful, clean | Confident, plain | Even beats | Most projects — the safe default |
| `polished` | Premium, restrained | Understated | Slower, deliberate | B2B tools, paid products |
| `cinematic` | Dramatic, trailer-scale | Sparse, weighty | Slow build, big final beat | A genuinely striking visual result |
| `chaotic` | High-energy, meme-adjacent | Loud, exclamatory | Fast cuts | Fun side projects, hackathon demos |
| `deadpan` | Flat, dry | Understated to the point of irony | Even, unhurried | Projects that are secretly very funny |

Do not force `cinematic` onto a utility script, or `chaotic` onto enterprise
software — mismatch between tone and subject reads as try-hard. Ask step 1's
question 6 honestly.

## Training mode

Training tone stays narrower on purpose — the job is clarity, not hype.

| Preset | Energy | Voice | Pacing | Use when |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `default` | Calm, clear | Plain, second person ("click X") | Even, generous | Most walkthroughs |
| `brisk` | Efficient | Plain, imperative | Faster, fewer pauses | Viewer already knows the basics |
| `patient` | Gentle | Plain, explains *why* not just *what* | Slower, more breathing room | Onboarding for non-technical viewers |

Never use marketing language ("game-changing", "revolutionary", "unlock") in
training-mode copy — it undermines trust in an instructional context.
